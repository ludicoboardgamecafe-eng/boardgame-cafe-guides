🔍 What Is Cluedo?

Cluedo (called Clue in North America) is the ultimate murder mystery board game. Imagine you're a detective in a grand mansion — someone has been murdered, and it's your job to figure out who did it, with what weapon, and in which room.

The game was invented by a British guy named Anthony Pratt way back in 1943 and has been a worldwide hit ever since. The Indian edition sold in India is published by Hasbro and features the classic Cluedo characters, rooms, and weapons — just like the original!

The Big Goal: Be the first player to correctly solve the mystery — name the right suspect, the right weapon, and the right room. Get all three right and you win! 🎉

It's a game of logic, deduction, and a little bit of bluffing. Perfect for people who love puzzles, whodunnit stories, or just outwitting their friends!

Before the game starts, help guests find all these pieces:

  • 🗺️ 1 Game Board — the mansion map with 9 rooms and hallways
  • 🪆 6 Suspect Tokens — coloured playing pieces
  • 🔫 6 Weapon Pieces — miniature props
  • 🃏 21 Cards — 6 Suspect cards + 6 Weapon cards + 9 Room cards
  • 📋 Detective Notepads — one per player for tracking clues
  • ✉️ 1 Secret Envelope (Solve Envelope) — holds the 3 mystery cards
  • 🎲 2 Dice

🎬 Setting Up the Game
  1. 1
    Lay out the board in the middle of the table so everyone can reach it.
  2. 2
    Sort the cards into 3 separate piles: Suspects, Weapons, and Rooms.
  3. 3
    Shuffle each pile separately — keep them face down so nobody peeks!
  4. 4
    Slip one card from each pile into the secret Solve Envelope — without looking. Seal it and place it in the centre of the board. These 3 cards are the answer!
  5. 5
    Shuffle the remaining 18 cards together and deal them out (face down) to all players as equally as possible. Some players might get one extra card — that's fine!
  6. 6
    Place the suspect tokens on their starting squares shown on the board. Place all 6 tokens, even if not all are being played.
  7. 7
    Place one weapon in each room at random. You can spread them out however you like!
  8. 8
    Give everyone a Detective Notepad and a pencil. Everyone looks at their own cards privately and marks them off on the notepad.
  9. 9
    Decide who goes first — everyone rolls the dice and the highest roller starts. Play goes clockwise.
🎲 How to Play — Your Turn

Each turn has up to 3 phases:

  1. 1
    Roll the Dice & Move
    Roll both dice and move your token that many spaces on the board. You can go up, down, left, right — but never diagonally. You cannot pass through other tokens. You can enter a room through its doorway (the gap in the wall).
  2. 2
    Make a Suggestion (if you're in a room)
    Once inside a room, you can suggest who you think did it, with which weapon, in this room. Say it out loud: e.g., "I suggest it was Mrs. Peacock, in the Library, with the Candlestick!"

    Now move the named suspect token and weapon piece into that room (even if they were elsewhere). The player to your left must look at their cards and show you ONE card from their hand that matches any part of your suggestion (suspect, weapon, or room) — privately. If they have no matching cards, the next player to the left checks, and so on around the table. The first person with a matching card shows it only to you.
  3. 3
    Make an Accusation (optional, when you're sure!)
    When you're confident you know all three answers, you can make a Final Accusation on your turn. You can accuse any room — you don't need to be in it. Secretly check the Solve Envelope. If you're right — you win! Show everyone the cards as proof. If you're wrong — you're out of the game, but you keep playing to answer other people's suggestions (you just can't make any more suggestions or accusations yourself).
🚀 Secret Passage Shortcut: If your token is in a corner room with a secret passage, you can use it instead of rolling — just move your token to the connected room. Then make a suggestion from there!
🏠 Staying in a Room: If you're already in a room and don't want to leave, you can stay and make a suggestion from there again. But you can't use the same room twice in a row on your own turns without leaving and re-entering.
📋 Using the Detective Notepad

The notepad is your best friend! It lists all suspects, weapons, and rooms. Here's how to use it:

✅ Cross off cards you hold: The cards in your own hand are definitely NOT in the envelope. Cross them off immediately.
✅ Cross off cards shown to you: When another player shows you a card, that card is not in the envelope. Cross it off!
✅ Watch what others suggest: Even when it's not your turn, pay attention! If Player A suggests Colonel Mustard, and Player B shows a card, you can start guessing what B showed based on context.
✅ Process of elimination: Once you've eliminated all but one suspect, weapon, and room — you know the answer! Make your accusation.
🏆 Winning & Losing
To Win: Make a Final Accusation on your turn. Secretly open the envelope and check the 3 cards. If your suspect, weapon, AND room all match — you win! Show everyone the cards as proof.
If You're Wrong: You're eliminated from making moves/suggestions. But you still show cards to others when they make suggestions — so keep paying attention! The game continues with remaining players.

The game ends when someone correctly solves the mystery or all players have made wrong accusations (in which case the mystery remains unsolved — reveal the cards so everyone sees the answer).

🧠 Smart Strategy Tips (Tell Your Guests!)
🎯 Suggest your own cards: Suggest things you already know — like a weapon card you're holding. If someone shows that card to disprove it, you learn nothing new. But you confuse the other players who don't know you have it!
🗺️ Move efficiently: Try to visit rooms you haven't eliminated yet. Plan your path — rooms with secret passages let you cover two rooms in two turns without much dice luck.
👀 Watch who shows cards: If you suggest Colonel Mustard and the player to your left passes (shows nothing), but the third player shows a card — you know player 2 holds none of: Mustard, your weapon, or your room. That's useful info!
⚡ Don't rush the accusation: One wrong accusation and you're basically out. Only accuse when you're 100% sure of all three answers!
❓ Common Guest Questions — FAQ
Can I make an accusation from any room?
Yes! A Final Accusation can name any room — you don't need to be inside it. Suggestions (the regular turn action) must match the room you're currently in.
What if nobody can disprove my suggestion?
If every other player checks and none of them have a matching card, great news — at least two of your three guesses are probably correct! Take careful notes. You're getting closer.
Can I suggest my own character?
Absolutely! Your character can be the murderer too. Suggest yourself to throw others off the scent.
Do I have to move every turn?
If you're already in a room, you can choose not to roll and not to move — but then you can't make a suggestion that turn either. You must move to make a suggestion. Alternatively, use a secret passage instead of rolling!
What if two people have the same number of cards?
That's completely normal! When 18 cards are dealt to 3–6 players, some get one more than others. It doesn't affect the game.
Can we look at the envelope before the game ends?
Only when someone makes a Final Accusation — and only that player looks (secretly). No peeking during the game!
Is this the same as the classic Cluedo?
Yes! The Indian edition sold in India has the same classic characters, weapons, and gameplay as the original. The Hasbro version available here follows standard Cluedo rules.
