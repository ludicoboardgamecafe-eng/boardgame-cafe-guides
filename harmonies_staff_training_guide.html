Harmonies Staff Training Guide

For board game café staff in Kochi — casual, clear, and guest-friendly.

1. What is Harmonies?

Harmonies is a beautiful nature-building strategy game. Players take colourful tokens, place them on their personal board, create landscapes like trees, mountains, fields, rivers, and buildings, and try to attract animals by making the right habitat patterns.

The game feels peaceful and pretty, but there is a lot of clever planning. Staff can describe it as: “Build a tiny ecosystem, make habitats for animals, and score points from both landscapes and animals.”

Players: 1–4
Time: 30–45 minutes
Difficulty: Light-medium
Best for: Couples, families, calm strategy lovers

2. What staff should know first

Harmonies has three main ideas:

  1. Take tokens: Pick one group of 3 tokens from the central board.
  2. Place tokens: Put all 3 tokens onto your personal board, following placement rules.
  3. Complete animal habitats: If your board matches an animal card pattern, place animal cubes for points.
Staff tip: New players usually understand the game faster when you teach the landscape scoring first, then animal cards second.

3. Components

  • Central board where token groups are displayed
  • Personal boards for each player
  • Coloured tokens: trees, mountains, water, fields, buildings, etc.
  • Animal cards
  • Animal cubes
  • Reminder cards
  • Pouch for random token draw
  • Score sheet
  • Optional Nature’s Spirit cards for advanced games

4. Goal of the game

Score the most points by:

  • Building valuable landscapes on your board
  • Completing animal habitat patterns
  • Placing animal cubes from your animal cards
  • Using your board space efficiently

At the end, players add landscape points and animal card points. Highest score wins.

5. Setup

  1. Place the central board in the middle.
  2. Put all tokens into the pouch.
  3. Fill each central board space with 3 random tokens.
  4. Shuffle animal cards and reveal 5 face-up cards.
  5. Keep animal cubes nearby.
  6. Each player takes one personal board and one reminder card.
  7. Use the same board side for all players. For beginners, use Side A.
  8. Choose a first player and point the central board arrow toward them.
Café recommendation: For a first game, do not use Nature’s Spirit cards. Add them only after players are comfortable.

6. Turn structure

On your turn, you must take and place tokens. You may also take an animal card and place animal cubes if possible. These actions can happen in a flexible order.

7. Placing tokens — simple explanation

You place tokens on your personal board to create landscapes. Some tokens can be stacked; some have special rules. Always follow the player board and reminder card.

  • Water: Usually placed flat to make rivers or water groups.
  • Fields: Yellow tokens often score by groups.
  • Trees: Green tokens can form trees by stacking with trunk pieces depending on rules.
  • Mountains: Grey tokens can stack to create height.
  • Buildings: Red/brown/grey-style structures help with animal patterns and scoring.
Important: Staff should use the reminder card while teaching. Harmonies is visual, so pointing at the symbols is easier than over-explaining.

8. Animal cards and habitats

Animal cards show small patterns. If your personal board matches a pattern, you may place one animal cube from that card onto the correct token shown by the card.

Key rules for animals

  • You can hold up to 4 incomplete animal cards.
  • When you take an animal card, place its animal cubes on the card.
  • Each time you complete the habitat pattern, move the lowest available cube from that card to your board.
  • The animal cube must go on the token indicated by the card.
  • One token can hold only one animal cube.
  • Patterns may usually be rotated or mirrored unless the card says otherwise.
  • Once placed, an animal cube stays there.
  • When all cubes from a card are placed, move the completed card aside. It no longer counts toward your 4-card limit.
Guest-friendly line: “Animal cards are like little missions. Make this shape on your board, then the animal moves in.”

9. Scoring overview

Scoring has two big parts:

  1. Landscape scoring: Points from the landscapes you built.
  2. Animal scoring: Points from animal cubes you successfully placed.

Animal cards usually show a descending score track. The earlier cubes placed from that card may be worth more points. At the end, score only the animal cubes that were placed on your board.

Common mistake: Players sometimes score animals still sitting on cards. Don’t do that. Only placed animals score.

10. End of game

The game ends when one of these happens:

  • A player has 2 or fewer empty spaces left on their personal board at the end of their turn.
  • The pouch is empty and the central board cannot be fully refilled.

Finish according to the rulebook timing, then score everyone’s boards. Highest total wins.

11. How to teach Harmonies in 3 minutes

“In Harmonies, each of us is building a small natural landscape. On your turn, pick one group of 3 tokens from the center and place all 3 on your board. These tokens create forests, mountains, water, fields, and buildings. You can also take animal cards. Each animal card shows a habitat pattern. If your board matches that pattern, the animal moves in and scores points. At the end, you score your landscapes plus the animals you placed. Most points wins.”

12. Common guest questions

Can I take fewer than 3 tokens?
No. You take and place all 3 tokens from one central space.
Can I move tokens later?
No. Once placed, tokens stay there.
Can I rotate animal patterns?
Usually yes. The pattern can be matched in different orientations unless a specific card/rule says otherwise.
Can two animals use the same habitat?
The same landscape arrangement can help with multiple cards, but a single token can hold only one animal cube.
Do unplaced animal cubes score?
No. Only animal cubes placed on your personal board score.
Should beginners use Nature’s Spirit cards?
No. Start without them. Add them for experienced players.

13. Common mistakes to watch for

  • Taking an animal card when already holding 4 incomplete cards.
  • Forgetting to refill the central board space after a turn.
  • Placing animal cubes on the wrong token from the card pattern.
  • Scoring unplaced animal cubes.
  • Forgetting that animal cubes stay even if the landscape changes later.
  • Running out of board space because the player placed tokens randomly early on.

14. Staff facilitation tips

  • Teach with one sample turn. Harmonies is easier to learn by seeing it.
  • Tell guests not to worry about perfect scoring in the first game.
  • Point out animal cards that are easy to complete early.
  • Remind players to leave space for future patterns.
  • For families, focus on building pretty landscapes first; explain deeper strategy after round one.
  • For strategy gamers, mention the puzzle of choosing token sets and timing animal cards.
